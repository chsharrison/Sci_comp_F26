Overview

This dashboard visualizes the impacts of nuclear winter scenarios on global climate, agriculture, and food security. All data are derived from climate model simulations and agricultural impact assessments based on stratospheric soot injection scenarios.

Data Sources

  • Temperature & Precipitation: Climate model outputs from WACCM6 (Whole Atmosphere Community Climate Model version 6) simulations
  • Agriculture: CLM crop yield projections (wheat, corn, rice, soy, pasture grass) based on climate disruption
  • Fisheries: Marine catch data from global fishery models under altered ocean conditions
  • Starvation: Food availability calculations based on Xia et al. (2022) methodology

Nuclear Winter Scenarios

Scenarios defined by mass of black carbon (soot) injected into stratosphere:

  • 0 Tg: Control scenario (no nuclear conflict)
  • 5, 16, 27, 37, 47 Tg: India-Pakistan regional conflict scenarios with varying weapon counts and yields
  • 150 Tg: U.S.-Russia superpower conflict scenario (4400 weapons × 100 kt each)

Starvation Methodology

Source: Xia, L., Robock, A., Scherrer, K. et al. "Global food insecurity and famine from reduced crop, marine fishery and livestock production due to climate disruption from nuclear war soot injection." Nature Food 3, 586–596 (2022). DOI: 10.1038/s43016-022-00573-0

Key Assumptions:

  • All stored food consumed within months post-war
  • Available food distributed to provide minimum calories for regular physical activity
  • Once food exhausted, no food provided to predicted deaths
  • Three sub-scenario dimensions: Trade (trade vs. no trade), Livestock (livestock/partial/none), Waste reduction (0%, 25%, 50%, 100%)

Outputs:

  • Percent starving (%): Percentage of country population unable to obtain minimum caloric requirements
  • Number starving: Absolute number of people (millions) facing starvation in each scenario

Note: The specific caloric threshold (kcal/day) used to define "starving" is not specified in publicly accessible sources. Methodology references "minimum number of calories needed to support regular physical activity" but exact values may be country-specific. Consult supplementary materials or contact study authors for precise thresholds.

Data Interpretation Notes

  • Years elapsed: Time since nuclear detonation (year 0 = conflict year, years 1-10 = post-war period)
  • Crop yield changes: Percent change relative to control (0 Tg) scenario for same time period
  • Missing data: Some countries/scenarios may have incomplete data due to model limitations or negligible impacts
  • Uncertainties: All projections subject to model uncertainties, climate variability, and assumptions about post-war human behavior

About the Nuclear Winter Data Commons

This dashboard was built using data from the Nuclear Winter Data Commons (NWDC), a repository of simplified, spreadsheet-ready datasets derived from established Earth System Model simulations. The full data includes variables relevant to both human and environmental impacts of nuclear conflict: temperature, precipitation, ultraviolet radiation, crop yields, fish catch, and sea ice thickness for a range of nuclear conflict scenarios. This repository aims to facilitate interdisciplinary research into the long-term consequences of nuclear detonations.

Nuclear winter refers to the suite of physical and biological consequences that may follow nuclear conflict, particularly the cooling and darkening of Earth’s surface due to black carbon soot in the upper atmosphere. While the associated changes in temperature, precipitation, and food system productivity have been the subject of climate modeling for decades, the outputs of models used to project these effects are stored in large files with formats unfamiliar to the broader research community. This paper introduces a standardized, user-friendly repository of simulated nuclear conflict climate impact data designed to lower barriers for non-specialist researchers.

About the Scenarios

Nuclear Winter scenarios are defined by the quantity of black carbon (soot) injected into the stratosphere by fires ignited during nuclear conflict, measured in teragrams (Tg). More soot = more sunlight blocked = more cooling and agricultural disruption.

Sub-Scenario Dimensions (Starvation Data Only)

Trade Status

  • With Trade: Countries can import food from regions less affected by nuclear winter.
  • No Trade: Each country relies only on its own food production — a worst-case assumption.

Livestock

  • Livestock: Grain is diverted to maintain livestock herds (less grain available for human consumption).
  • Partial Livestock: Partial livestock culling; some grain redirected to human food.
  • No Livestock: All grain previously used for livestock is redirected to human food.

Food Waste Reduction

  • 0% Reduction: No change in food waste behavior.
  • 50% Reduction: Half of normally wasted food is consumed instead.
  • 100% Reduction: All food waste eliminated (theoretical maximum efficiency).

Note: The current dataset includes only the “0% reduction” waste scenario. The 50% and 100% reduction options are reserved for future data releases.
